<p>Post Falls is not just the place that we live. It is the place that we are called to. Kootenai County, the inland northwest — it is not just a place on the map, it is a place we are praying people fall in love with Jesus.</p><p>That was the frame around this Sunday's message, and it wasn't sentimental. All around us are people whose lives look like they're going great, who are carrying pain nobody else gets to see. Marriages breaking apart. Parents watching kids make unhealthy decisions. Addiction. Shame. Anxiety, fear, bitterness, despair, loneliness — inside our community and inside our church. There are people here who have almost given up on themselves. But Jesus has not given up on them.</p><p>Which is exactly why Acts 16 matters so much. Because a church service can gather people, but the gospel is out there to change people's lives. Acts 16 shows us four things to expect when it does.</p><h2>1. Chains Start Breaking</h2><p>Paul, Silas, and Luke are in Philippi, walking to the place of prayer, when a slave girl with a spirit of divination starts following them. Luke includes a devastating detail: she brought her owners much gain. They were making significant money from her torment.</p><p>They didn't see her as a young woman who needed help. They saw her as a source of income. They didn't see her pain, they saw her as profit. As it was put on Sunday: "They didn't see her as a person. They saw her as a product." Her owner saw a business opportunity, but Jesus saw a captive to be set free.</p><p>Paul and Silas weren't looking for a confrontation. There are moments you go looking for ministry and there are moments ministry finds you — and when the gospel enters a city, eventually it confronts whatever is holding people captive.</p><p>But before the chain broke out there, something had to break in here. The honest warning was that we can walk by bondage for so long that we stop noticing it, until we assume that's just the way Post Falls is, that's just the way Kootenai County is, that family has always been broken. "When the Holy Spirit opens your eyes you stop accepting bondage as normal." And: "Before Jesus begins breaking using us to break chains, we have to have something break within us."</p><p>Then notice the method. No elaborate ceremony. No negotiating. No asking how long it had been there. Just: I command you in the name of Jesus Christ to come out of her. The gospel does not negotiate with chains; it announces the authority of Jesus and breaks them.</p><h2>2. Darkness Starts Fighting</h2><p>You would think a city would celebrate a freed girl. Instead her owners saw that their hope of gain was gone and dragged Paul and Silas to the magistrates — and changed the charge. It stopped being about money and became about Jews disturbing the city and advocating unlawful customs.</p><p>Sit with the tragedy of that phrase for a second. Their hope was not built upon Jesus. Their hope was built upon keeping another human being in bondage. Sometimes you don't know what somebody truly worships until Jesus starts breaking chains.</p><p>Here's the North Idaho picture that made it stick. Anyone raised around this much water knows you can't sit by a creek without picking up a rock. Flip one over and bugs scatter. One side of that rock looked totally normal, completely peaceful. Everything that had been comfortable in the darkness starts looking for a place to hide.</p><p>The gospel turned over the rock in Philippi. It did not put the darkness there — it was already there. Some people do not mind the church as long as the church stays within the walls and remains quiet. But when chains begin to break, when addicts begin to walk in freedom, when marriages begin to heal, when young people find their identity in Jesus — darkness gets nervous. "Darkness does not fight because the gospel is weak." It fights when the gospel is working.</p><h2>3. Praise Starts Rising</h2><p>About midnight. Luke chose that phrase carefully — the crowd has gone home, the magistrates are sleeping, and Paul and Silas are sitting in a dark, secure prison with bruised backs, bleeding wounds, and feet in stocks.</p><p>Midnight is the moment yesterday is over but tomorrow has not fully arrived. It's a season when you have prayed but you are still sitting in the prison. When you've obeyed but you're still hurting. When you believe God is faithful but your circumstances have not changed. And midnight is where your faith has to rise and decide that God is still worthy even though morning has not arrived.</p><p>They had every reason to complain. Instead they prayed and sang hymns — while their wounds hurt, while their feet were in stocks, knowing they could be executed when morning came. Not because the earthquake had happened yet. Not because the chains had fallen off. They praised because God was still God. Psalm 34 says it: I will bless the Lord at all times. All times includes mountaintops and prisons, celebrations and suffering. If he is God in the daylight, he is the God of midnight also.</p><p>Consider the birds. Sitting on a deck before sunrise this week, looking at the mountains with a Bible open, the sky still dark — and the birds in the neighbor's pine trees start singing anyway, because something inside them knows the darkness won't last forever. "Praise is faith singing in the dark because it knows midnight will not have the final word."</p><p>And then five quiet words: the prisoners were listening to them. Someone is always listening to how you respond at midnight. Your children. Your family. Your coworkers and neighbors. Sometimes they aren't listening to what you say about God when life is good; they're listening to what you say about God when life hurts. "Your worship in the darkness may become someone else's introduction to the light." When praise started rising, the prison shook and everyone's bonds came off. Praise rose from two people, and the freedom reached everyone around them.</p><h2>4. Households Start Believing</h2><p>The earthquake wakes the jailer. He sees open doors, assumes the prisoners are gone, and draws his sword on himself — under Roman rule he was personally responsible for the men in his care, and his own family could have paid too.</p><p>This may be one of the most incredible moments in Scripture. Paul had an open door and every human reason to take it. He stays. Do not harm yourself, we are all here. Sometimes God opens the door so that you can see the other person standing on the other side. Paul's opportunity for escape became the jailer's opportunity for salvation.</p><p>Notice too that Paul was not yet whole. Some of us think we have to be completely free of our own pain before God can use us — but you do not have to be completely free of your own pain for God to use you to touch someone else's situation.</p><p>What must I do to be saved? Believe in the Lord Jesus. Salvation is not what you accomplish; it is found in Jesus and putting your faith in him. He lived, he took the punishment our sins deserved, he rose — and anyone who places their faith in him can be forgiven, restored, and walk a new life. The jailer didn't keep it to himself. His household heard the word, he was baptized at once, and he rejoiced with his entire house. One man's midnight became an entire family's salvation. Even for families that have walked through dark things, when Jesus enters the home he begins writing a completely different story.</p><p>So that's what we're praying over Post Falls, Kootenai County, and the whole inland northwest — not just individual decisions, but whole households. Husbands and wives following Jesus together. Parents and children worshiping as one family. Generational chains broken. Homes filled with prayer, grace, forgiveness, and the Word of God. God may begin with you, but he does not have to stop with you.</p><p>That's also why we ended at the water. Romans 6:4 — we were buried with him by baptism into death, so that as Christ was raised, we too might walk in newness of life. Every baptism at City Beach is somebody publicly confessing that they are a child of God, and every one of them is evidence the gospel is moving in our city.</p><p>Watch the full message and find service times at <a href="https://theriverag.church">theriverag.church</a>. If you want to be baptized, tell us — we would love to celebrate with you.</p>
